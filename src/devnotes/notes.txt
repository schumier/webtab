Yes. I would calculate them in stages, because Deviation, Normal Range, Consecutive Direction, and Trend are related but measure different things.

Suppose one UIM has these month-end duplicate counts:

ME	Duplicate

Apr	100
May	105
Jun	98
Jul	102
Aug	140


We are evaluating August.

1. Deviation from 3M Average %

Use the three completed month-ends before the current month, so don't include August itself.

3M average:

\[
Avg3M = \frac{May+Jun+Jul}{3}
\]

\[
=\frac{105+98+102}{3}=101.67
\]

Then:

\[
Deviation\%=
\frac{Current-Avg3M}{Avg3M}\times100
\]

For August:

\[
\frac{140-101.67}{101.67}\times100
=\mathbf{+37.7\%}
\]

Meaning:

> August is 37.7% above its recent 3-month average.



This is useful, but +37.7% by itself doesn't tell you whether that movement is statistically unusual.


---

2. Historical Normal Range

This is where you measure how much this particular UIM normally fluctuates.

I would not use only 3 months for this. If available, use perhaps the previous 12 month-ends.

A simple first method is:

\[
Historical\ Mean \pm 2 \times Standard\ Deviation
\]

Suppose the previous 12 ME values produce:

Historical Mean = 101

Standard Deviation = 6

Then:

\[
Lower=101-(2\times6)=89
\]

\[
Upper=101+(2\times6)=113
\]

So:

> Historical Normal Range = 89–113



August = 140

Therefore:

> 🔴 Above historical normal range



This is much stronger evidence than merely saying August increased from July.

Important for your data

Because duplicate/null data may have historical spikes, I'd eventually test a median/MAD-based range, which is less distorted by outliers. But mean ± 2 SD is much easier for your first prototype and for explaining the concept to your team.


---

3. Consecutive Direction

This doesn't need statistics. Look at the direction of recent month-end movements.

For example:

May 100 → Jun 110 → Jul 125 → Aug 150

Comparisons:

Jun > May ↑
Jul > Jun ↑
Aug > Jul ↑

So:

> 3 consecutive increases ↑↑↑



Another UIM:

150 → 130 → 115 → 100

becomes:

> 3 consecutive decreases ↓↓↓



And:

100 → 130 → 105 → 140

becomes:

> Mixed / alternating ↑↓↑



I would store two things internally:

direction = UP / DOWN / MIXED / FLAT

and

consecutive_periods = 3

Then Streamlit can display:

> ↑ Increasing 3 ME



instead of exposing the technical calculation.


---

4. Trend

Now combine the above signals.

This is where I'd avoid making Current > Previous automatically mean Deteriorating.

For a metric where higher = worse, such as duplicate count:

🔴 Deteriorating

Current is materially above expected behaviour and/or showing a sustained adverse movement.

For example:

3M Avg = 102
Current = 140
Deviation = +37%
Historical range = 89–113
Consecutive direction = ↑ 3 ME

→ Deteriorating

🟢 Improving

Suppose:

180 → 155 → 130 → 110

and historical normal is 90–115.

Current has moved substantially downward and is approaching/returning to its normal range.

→ Improving

⚪ Stable

Suppose:

100 → 103 → 99 → 102

Historical range 90–113.

Small movements, all within normal historical variability.

→ Stable

🟠 Volatile

Suppose:

100 → 150 → 90 → 145

Large movements in opposite directions.

→ Volatile

Putting everything together

You could have this in your backend analysis table:

UIM	Current	Prev	3M Avg	Dev vs 3M	Normal Range	Direction	Trend

UIM-A	140	102	101.7	+37.7%	89–113	↑ 1	🔴 Deteriorating
UIM-B	108	105	103.0	+4.9%	90–115	↑ 2	⚪ Stable
UIM-C	110	130	145.0	-24.1%	90–115	↓ 3	🟢 Improving
UIM-D	145	90	113.3	+28.0%	85–120	Mixed	🟠 Volatile


Notice UIM-B is increasing for two periods, but it's still classified Stable because the movement remains within its normal historical variability.

That's why I wouldn't derive Trend from consecutive direction alone.

One improvement I'd make for your ~1,000 UIMs

I'd calculate a standardized deviation as well:

\[
Z = \frac{Current-HistoricalMean}{HistoricalSD}
\]

This gives you a common scale across very different UIM volumes.

For example:

UIM A: Z = +0.4 → quite normal
UIM B: Z = +2.3 → unusual
UIM C: Z = +5.1 → extremely unusual

Even if one UIM normally has 100 records and another normally has 1,000,000 records, you can compare how unusual each one is relative to its own history.

I wouldn't necessarily show the term Z-score prominently to users. You can use it behind the scenes and display something understandable like:

> Within Expected Range
Above Expected Range
Significantly Above Expected Range



That could ultimately be very useful for your Top UIMs requiring attention ranking.












For Issue Pattern, I would determine it from the historical sequence of whether each month-end was outside or inside the expected range.

So first, for every UIM/month-end, create:

Exception = Y → outside your defined normal/tolerance range
Exception = N → within expected range

Then New/Persistent/Recurring becomes straightforward.

Example

Assume we're evaluating August:

UIM	May	Jun	Jul	Aug	Issue Pattern

A	N	N	N	Y	🆕 New
B	N	Y	Y	Y	⚠️ Persistent
C	Y	N	N	Y	🔄 Recurring
D	Y	Y	Y	N	✅ Resolved
E	N	N	N	N	Normal


New

Current ME is an exception, but there was no exception in the historical lookback window.

Example:

N → N → N → Y

→ New

Meaning:

> “This UIM has newly moved outside its expected range.”




---

Persistent

Current ME is an exception and the immediately preceding ME was also an exception.

Example:

N → Y → Y → Y

→ Persistent — 3 consecutive ME

You can calculate the consecutive count:

> Persistent 2M
Persistent 3M
Persistent 6M



This is more useful than just saying Persistent.


---

Recurring

Current ME is an exception, the previous ME(s) returned to normal, but an older exception exists.

Example:

Y → N → N → Y

→ Recurring

Or:

Y → N → Y → N → Y

→ Recurring

Meaning:

> “We've seen this abnormal behaviour before, it normalized, and now it has returned.”



This is where your previous Hive observations become especially valuable.


---

Resolved

Previous ME was an exception, but current ME is back within expected range:

Y → Y → Y → N

→ Resolved

You may want to keep Resolved visible for one month-end and then stop showing it as an active pattern afterward.

One important decision: historical lookback

You need to decide how far back counts as “New”.

Suppose:

Jan-24 = Exception
then normal for 18 months
Aug-26 = Exception

Technically it occurred before, but calling August 2026 Recurring because something happened 18+ months ago may not be useful.

I'd therefore define a lookback, perhaps:

> Pattern lookback = previous 6 or 12 month-ends



Then:

No exception within lookback + current Y → New

Previous ME Y + current Y → Persistent

Previous ME N + an earlier Y within lookback + current Y → Recurring

The appropriate 6/12 months should be agreed based on your UIM behaviour.
